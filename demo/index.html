LoRa accident detection · two AI models on an ESP32 + MPU6050

Crash, pothole or speed breaker?

Every jolt above 0.45 g wakes two models on the device. Model A decides whether it was a crash, from 30 accelerometer and 7 gyroscope features — trained on VZCrash, 31,000 real crashes recorded on the road. Model B then names what the jolt actually was: a speed breaker, a pothole, harsh driving or nothing. Everything below runs on data the models never saw.

How to read these results

  • The crashes are real. VZCrash contains 16 s of 100 Hz IMU data per event, labelled by three trained reviewers with dashcam footage. Nothing about the crash class is simulated or assembled.
  • The negatives are hard on purpose. VZCrash's non-crash events were selected as interesting — near misses, potholes, harsh braking, off-road manoeuvres. A 2.4% false-alarm rate here is not 2.4% of driving time; on ordinary recorded driving (the PVS drives) this system raises no crash alert at all over 200 s and 119 jolts.
  • The trigger is part of the detector. Events that never reach 0.45 g are never classified, and count as misses in every number here.
  • Model B is the weaker half. Speed bumps and rough road are labelled per drive in PVS, not per event, and only 95 bump crossings exist. It tells a bump from a crash reliably, and a bump from rough road about half the time.
